Common Estate Planning Mistakes

Avoid common oversights that can create confusion for loved ones.

Mistake 1: Not having a plan

When there is no plan, state law and court processes may determine what happens. This can create delays, expense, and family stress.

Mistake 2: Not updating beneficiaries

Life insurance, retirement accounts, and payable-on-death accounts often pass by beneficiary designation. Old designations can override what a person assumed would happen.

Mistake 3: Creating documents but not funding a trust

A trust that does not own or control assets may not deliver the probate-avoidance benefits the family expected.

Mistake 4: Ignoring incapacity

Planning is not only about death. Powers of attorney, healthcare directives, and trustee succession can be essential if someone becomes unable to act.

Mistake 5: Not telling anyone where documents are

Even a good plan can fail if loved ones cannot find it. Keep a clear organizer with contacts, accounts, policies, and document locations.

Long-term care can affect the entire family

A care event can change retirement cash flow, family roles, housing decisions, and the assets you intended to preserve for a spouse or heirs.

Protect Choices

Planning can create more options for where and how care is received, subject to policy benefits and eligibility.

Protect Family

Insurance benefits may reduce the amount of hands-on care or financial support family members must provide.

Protect the Plan

A dedicated funding strategy can help keep a prolonged care need from consuming assets intended for retirement or legacy goals.

Ways people prepare

  • Traditional long-term care insurance
  • Life insurance with qualifying long-term care or chronic-illness benefits
  • Asset-based or hybrid insurance strategies
  • Self-funding with earmarked assets
  • Coordinating insurance with family and estate planning

What to compare

  • Benefit amount and benefit period
  • Elimination or waiting periods
  • Inflation protection
  • Covered care settings and benefit triggers
  • Premium structure and carrier strength
  • What happens if care is never needed

Common questions

Clear answers before you make a decision.

Does Medicare pay for long-term custodial care?

Medicare has limited coverage for qualifying skilled care and is not designed to pay indefinitely for ongoing custodial long-term care. Eligibility rules and circumstances matter.

When should I consider long-term care planning?

Planning before a care need develops generally provides more options because insurance eligibility depends on health and underwriting.

What is a hybrid LTC policy?

Hybrid or asset-based strategies combine life insurance or another insurance contract with benefits that may be available for qualifying long-term care or chronic illness, depending on the product and rider terms.
